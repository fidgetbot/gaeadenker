Gaea Denker

Writer, Playwright, Filmmaker, and All-Around Story Collector

I'd use the term "Renaissance Woman," but that would imply I'm toothless and on my tenth child and that simply isn't true.

My debut novel is currently on submission with Brandi Bowles of United Talent Agency. It's women's fiction with a speculative twist. Hopefully, you'll get a chance to read it soon.

About Me

I write for newspapers, magazines, radio, theatrical productions, and independent film. My work has been performed in theaters in San Francisco, New York, and Boston, while my short fiction has earned national recognition in competitions including the James White Award Short Story Competition and the Parsec Short Story Contest.

I've written for publications and productions including The Tallahassee Democrat, The Nob Hill Gazette, Fayette Woman, Deep South Magazine, and the 915 Cayuga live radio variety show.

I also work behind the camera, creating documentary videos about people whose lives don't fit neatly into a headline: newly arrived refugee families, human-rights lawyers, Ukrainian mothers, survivors of torture, organic farmers, people undergoing bariatric surgery, and a 105-year-old Holocaust survivor.

Documentary Film

Post-Bypass

Directing, filming, editing

A documentary following the lives of people undergoing bariatric surgery and their experiences with life after extreme weight loss. This documentary is shown to prospective gastric-bypass patients at the Cayuga Medical Center, New York.

Royal Southern Tier

Directing, filming, editing

This documentary follows several contestants in an Upstate New York drag queen pageant as they navigate life both onstage and offstage. It was incorporated into the curriculum of a Cornell University Human Development class.

Breaking the Silence

Sound recording

A human-rights documentary focusing on torture survivors and their civil rights. I helped interview survivors and assisted with sound design and in-field audio recording during principal photography in Washington, D.C. The project involved Torture Abolition and Survivors Support Coalition International (TASSC), which was founded by Dianna Ortiz, an American nun who survived kidnapping and torture in Guatemala.
